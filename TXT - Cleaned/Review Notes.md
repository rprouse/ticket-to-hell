# Ticket to Hell via Dieppe — cleanup notes

These files are a cleaned copy of the TXT folder. The originals in TXT are untouched. Every doubtful reading was checked against the scans by re-OCRing all 199 scanned pages and, where needed, looking at the page image.

## What was changed

### Transcription errors (text did not match the printed book)

About 290 fixes. The main kinds:

- **Letter/number confusion:** `cou1dn’t`, `tl1e`, `O4:00`, `June rr` (11), `ztst` (21st), `t79` (179), `x6§` (16½), `IIS` (115), `April t 1` (11), `G0ethe`, `Hit1er`.
- **f/fl/fi misreads:** `Hash`→flash, `Hat car`/`fiat car`→flat car, `Bight`→flight, `Hue`→flue, `Held`→field, `Ending`→finding, `list`→fist, `linger`→finger, `dying suit`→flying suit, `dew`→flew, `{ire`→fire, `tires`→fires.
- **"fighter" read as "lighter"** (three times): "our fighter planes", "fighter escorts", "bomber and fighter planes". The same fault turned "pre-fight knee-bends" into "pre-light".
- **Other word misreads:** `machine-gum`, `comer`→corner, `yowls`→jowls, `wanting`→warning, `sewing my sentence`→serving, `marina`→manna, `mms`→runs, `tom cartilages`→torn, `garmlous`→garrulous, `bom scrounger`→born, `aims training`→arms training, `wrapped itself around one`→me, `tumbled`→rumbled, `Tonuny`→Tommy, `jack Hinton`→Jack Hinton, `bugger oil'`→bugger off, `what I are`→what I ate.
- **Garbled German or names:** `rischler` (Tischler), `arbeirers`, `kapuz`/`kapuf` (the book prints "kaput"), `_7a`/`]a` (Ja), `Zwzilager` (the book prints "Zweilager"), `Wafen-SS Tatenkapf`, `Schatz-Szajj’eln`, `Sickerheitsdiem:`, `Sraazspolizei`, `Valk Sturm`, `unter ojizier`, `Diisseldorf`, `Miinchen`, `Miihlhausen`, `Koln` (the book prints "Köln"), `Athal` (the book prints "Athol").
- **Punctuation:** em dashes inside compound words (`twenty—one`, `box—cars` and about 40 more) changed to hyphens. Stray spaces before `? ! ;` removed. Broken closing quotes such as `anyway}`, `people}` and `shot?` fixed.

### Out-of-place text removed

No caption text from the photos had leaked into the narrative. All the stray material came from the scan margins and gutters:

- Single stray characters at the start or middle of lines: `Q`, `I`, `p`, `E`, `g`, `l`, `t`, `W`, `A`, `V`, `` ` ``, `~`, `¤`, `{`, `}`, `\`, `·`, `_`. This was heaviest in chapter 7, from "I could see ‘Gee-guts’" to "Plank-face", and in chapter 8 during the wood-party pages.
- Mangled fragments: `{BSI of the summer of ,43` (rest of the summer of ’43), `Molsdorf E and`, `for l . criminal`, `very V thin`.
- Printed page numbers 185–189, plus `V · _ 186`, in End Notes.

### Paragraph structure

- **99 false paragraph breaks merged.** The earlier conversion started a new paragraph wherever a printed line happened to end in a full stop. I checked each break against the indentation in the scans and merged the ones that were not real paragraph starts. One example: "There was a mad scramble…" in chapter 9 is a continuation in the book, not a new paragraph.
- **Missing breaks restored:** "One of the other Canadians…" (chapter 7), "We awoke cold and stiff…" (chapter 9), "They insulted the intelligence…" (chapter 8) and "After the round-up…" (chapter 9). The notice text "TAKE YOUR CHOICE" also now has a line break after it.
- Line breaks inside paragraphs were joined. The prose notes in Barbed-Wire Ballads were reflowed so they match the chapters. The verse lines are unchanged.
- **End Notes rebuilt by hand from the scans.** The March 1945 air-raid table was unreadable and is now laid out in columns. I added section headings (German Orders, Air Raids, A Note on Photos…, Organized Compassion) to match the Contents page. The broken English in the German notices is authentic, so it was left alone. That includes "matchen", "boundarys", "expecially" and "paiement".

## Flagged but not changed

### Probably wrong; worth checking before deciding

- **"Stalag VIG in Arnoldsweiler"** (chapters 5 and 8). I'm fairly but not fully sure the camp at Arnoldsweiler was Stalag VI H, and that VI G was at Bonn-Duisdorf.
- **"Zweilager"** (chapter 6) is printed this way. It is probably "Zweiglager" (branch camp), but I'm not certain.
- **"Hottenhausen"** (chapter 9). I couldn't place this on the route between Ebeleben and Grossneuhausen. It may be a misspelling of another village.
- **"SS Vullendam"** (Epilogue) is probably the SS Volendam, a Holland America ship that carried war brides in 1945. I'm fairly confident.
- **"destroyer Revenge"** (Prologue). HMS Revenge was a battleship.
- **"Hof in Saxony"** (chapter 9). Hof is in Bavaria, just over the Saxon border.
- **"Rouen-sur-Seine"**. The city is simply Rouen.
- **The 51st Division** was mostly captured at St-Valéry-en-Caux, not Dunkirk. The book attributes them to Dunkirk several times.

### Internal inconsistencies in the book

- **December 1944 raids:** chapter 8 says 115, but the Air Raids table says 113.
- **January 1945 raids:** chapter 8 says the alarm "only sounded seventy-two times", but the table says 94.
- **March 1945:** chapter 8 says 235 hours and the table says 234 hrs 35 min. That's fine as a rounding.
- **Date order in chapter 8:** "the big news arrived on March 20" comes after events of the 26th. It was probably the 28th or 29th.
- **Arnhem:** chapter 8 has new Canadian paratroopers from Arnhem arriving "shortly" after the end of July 1944. Arnhem was in late September 1944.
- **The POW who walked out of the gate:** chapter 8 has him beaten with rifle butts and "bleeding from the ear". The ballad note gives him "a bullet wound in his ear" and says he was a prisoner "five long years", but June 1944 was four years after Dunkirk.
- **The dyed trousers:** chapter 4 describes them as "sickly reddish-brown", then later as "three pairs of purple pants". This may be intentional.
- **"Fritz" in chapter 7:** first described as a front-line veteran who was easy to get along with, later as a guard with a glass eye who was "on the take". These may be two different guards.
- **Captions:** a photo caption spells it "Stradtroda", but the text has Stadtroda. This only matters if the captions are ever transcribed.

### Signatures and terms in the German notices left as printed

"Signed Ges. Jahn" (probably *gez.*, meaning "signed"), "Ober Wehrmacht" (probably the Oberkommando der Wehrmacht), "Signed Knabu".
