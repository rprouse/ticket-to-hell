A Canadian soldier’s wartime memoir

Ticket to Hell via Dieppe

From a Prisoner’s Wartime Log, 1942–1945

A. Robert Prouse

Robert Prouse was one of 5000 Canadians who volunteered for the disastrous August 1942 raid on Dieppe. Pinned down as soon as they hit the beach, Prouse was one of the many Canadians captured that they spent the rest of the war in German prison camps.

In a secret diary, Prouse kept a faithful record of three years’ captivity. Drawn from that log, this account offers an unusually honest, accurate picture of captives and captors. Prouse tells of the horrors faced by those in the German camps, of his two escape attempts, of courage, bravery, tragedy—and the remarkable spirit of men trapped together under inhuman conditions.

Ticket to Hell is a gruelling account of war and captivity, and of the qualities of the human spirit that endure it.

About the book

On August 19, 1942, A. Robert Prouse landed at Dieppe with the Canadian Provost Corps. Captured on the beach, he spent the rest of the war as a prisoner of Stalag IX C in Germany, kept a secret log of what he saw, escaped twice, and was liberated in April 1945.

Ticket to Hell via Dieppe was published in hardback in 1982 and in paperback in 1983, and has long been out of print. This edition was transcribed from scans of the original by the author’s grandson, corrected against the printed pages, and released free so his account can be read and heard again.

  1. August 19, 1942 The raid on Dieppe. Taken prisoner on the beach.
  2. 1942–1945 Stalag IX C and its work camps: Molsdorf, Niederorschel, Mühlhausen and Bad Sulza.
  3. April 1945 Forced march, then liberation.
